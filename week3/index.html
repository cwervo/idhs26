Lecture: From Plotters to Receipts

  • The Pen Plotter: The earliest digital printing. Vector-based, mechanical, drawing lines rather than pixels.
    • The machine as a physical actor performing a task over time.
    • Explore: Vera Molnar's early generative plotter art
  • The Xerox Laser Printer: The shift to batch processing and the "page" paradigm. High-fidelity, document-focused, and the creation of the modern office workflow.
    • The Invention of WYSIWYG: "What You See Is What You Get" was invented at Xerox PARC specifically so the digital screen interface would perfectly mirror the printed artifact.
  • The Thermal Receipt Printer: Continuous roll, immediate, tactile, and transactional. Why this low-fidelity, 1-bit medium is perfect for spatial computing and persistent state.
    • Inspiration: Attention Receipts
  • Introduction to Calm Technology: Before our discussion, let's look at two ways digital information is pushed to the physical periphery.
    • The Ultimate Calm Interface: The Dangling String (Live Wire) by Natalie Jeremijenko (1995). An 8-foot string attached to a motor that twitched in the periphery of the room based on digital network traffic.
    • Tangible Notifications: BERG's Little Printer (2012). A friendly, cloud-connected thermal printer that printed tiny, personalized morning briefs and messages from friends.
  • Physical Artifacts: Why physical artifacts still matter in a digital-first workflow. Discuss situations where a physically printed artifact is more convenient than digital equivalents (tickets? menus? checklists?).

15 Minute Break

Discussion: Calm Computing & Physical Debug Logs

  • Calm Computing: Moving information from the center of our attention to the periphery. How can a printout act as a "calm" notification? (Referencing the Little Printer and Dangling String).
  • Physical Debug Logs: Using printers not just for transactions, but as a tangible notification system, an audit trail, or a physical memory of a digital event.

Design Activity: Speculative Tangible Outputs

  • The Challenge: In groups, pick a modern, abstract digital service (e.g., Spotify, a smart home thermostat, a Slack channel, your bank app).
  • The Task: Design a physical "receipt" or printout that this service would generate.
    • What is the essential information?
    • How do you use typography (font weight, scale, hierarchy, etc.) without color?
    • What is the physical lifecycle of this piece of paper? (Is it pinned to a board? Handed to a friend? Crumpled up?)
  • Output: Collaborate in a Figma file on the design of your hypothetical receipt.
