Lecture: Re-evaluating Weiser & System Integration

We've spent the last month building discrete inputs (cameras, ArUco markers) and outputs (receipt printers). Today, we look at the glue that binds them: the network and the context.

  • Part 1: The Evolution of "Invisible" Computing
    • Review: Weiser’s 1991 vision of computing disappearing into the background. Did it happen? (The smartphone vs. the smart environment).
    • The "Invisible" Interface: When the system anticipates intent without explicit UI.
      • Example: Amazon Go / Just Walk Out (The camera as the entire checkout interface).
      • Example: The Original Nest Thermostat (Learning behavior vs. manual scheduling).
    • The Pitfalls of Invisibility: What happens when the system is wrong, but you can't see the controls to fix it? (Discussing user agency and "Seamful Design").
  • Part 2: Contextual Awareness
    • How does a system know where it is and what is happening around it?
    • Location & Proximity:
      • Example: iBeacon & BLE (Bluetooth Low Energy) - How retail stores know you are standing in front of the shoe aisle.
      • Example: Apple's U1 Chip (Ultra Wideband) - The spatial awareness powering AirDrop and AirTags.
    • State & Environment: Sensors beyond the camera (RFID, light, temperature, accelerometers).
  • Part 3: How Physical Programs "Talk"
    • How do devices communicate when they don't share a screen?
    • The Network Topologies:
      • Client-Server: Everyone talks to a central brain (like our WebSockets activity).
      • Peer-to-Peer (P2P): Devices talk directly to each other (e.g., Apple's Mesh networking for "Find My").
    • Protocols: The languages devices use to share state.
      • MQTT: Lightweight messaging for IoT devices.
      • WebSockets: Real-time, bi-directional communication (what we will use today).
